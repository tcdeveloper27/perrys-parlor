Welcome, Partner

Perry's Parlor is the classic card game Spider Solitaire, dealt on green felt in an Old West saloon parlor. There's ragtime on the player piano, a cat asleep by the window, a barkeep named Mose, and a whole family of friendly spiders who drop by to celebrate when you win — but never every time.

This manual explains everything in plain words. You don't need to read it all before you play: the game is built so that nothing you do can break it, every move can be taken back, and help is always one button away. Read Getting Started and How to Play, then come back to the rest whenever you're curious.

Good to know The game saves by itself after every move. Close it whenever you like — your hand will be waiting.

1. Getting Started

On a Windows computer

  1. Double-click Perrys Parlor v2r13 Setup.exe (the numbers at the end are the version).
  2. Windows may show a blue box that says "Windows protected your PC." That only means the game isn't from a big company. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Click Install, then Finish (leave "Open Perry's Parlor now" ticked to start right away). A gold sheriff's star called Perry's Parlor appears on your desktop and in the Start menu.
  4. Double-click the star whenever you want to play.

There's also a no-install copy (Perrys Parlor v2r13.exe): double-click it to play straight away.

On an Android phone or tablet

  1. Once Perry's Parlor is in the Play Store: open the Play Store, search for Perry's Parlor, and tap Install.
  2. Tap Open, or find the gold star among your apps.
  3. Turn the phone sideways — the game plays in landscape, so the whole table fits.

The first time you open it

The barkeep asks what to call you. Type a name — "Mr. Perry", "Grandpa", anything you like — and click Pull Up a Chair. The game greets you by that name, writes it in the Ledger, and puts it in the headlines when you win.

Anyone else who plays can add their own name later, and everyone keeps their own scores (see Players and the Guest Book).

The Guest Book on the first visit

Getting a newer version later

On Windows, run the new Setup the same way — your name, scores, Ledger, photos and Spider Family Album are all kept. On Android, the Play Store updates the game by itself.

3. How to Play Spider

The goal: build each suit in order from King down to Ace, all in one column. A finished run of thirteen cards slides off the table on its own. Clear them all and you win. (The face cards are painted frontier folk — the King of Spades is Sheriff Perry.)

The rules, plainly

  • Moving a card: put a card on any card one higher — a 7 on an 8, a Jack on a Queen. The suit doesn't matter for a single card.
  • Moving a group: a run of cards that are in order and the same suit can move together. Mixed suits stay put until you sort them out.
  • Turning cards over: when you move the last face-up card off a column, the card underneath turns over by itself.
  • Empty columns: an empty column can take any card or any same-suit run. Empty columns are very handy — use them wisely.
  • Dealing: when you're out of good moves, click the deck to deal one new card onto every column. Every column needs at least one card before you can deal. (Relaxed Spider lets you deal any time.)
  • Finishing a suit: when a column holds a full run from King to Ace in one suit, it gathers up with a gold shine and a "ta-da!", and goes home.

Scoring

WhenScore
You start every hand with500 points
Each move, each deal, each Undo−1 point
Undoing the move that finished a suittakes its 100 back
Each suit you finish+100 points

So fewer moves means a higher score (it never shows less than 0). The brass tag under the pocket watch (the clock at the top right on a phone) times the hand: it starts with your first move or deal and pauses whenever a menu is open or you step away from the game.

A good habit Turn over face-down cards whenever you can, and try to build runs in one suit. In a 1-suit game every run is the same suit, which is why it's the easiest.

5. Help When You Need It

Moving cards

Click (or tap) a card and it jumps to the best place it can go. Or drag it wherever you like — the column it can land on lights up. If a card can't move, it gives a little shake.

Undo — as many times as you like

Changed your mind? Click Undo. It takes back moves and deals one at a time, all the way back to the start of the hand.

Hint — shows you a good move

A thick gold frame shows the cards to move, a gold arrow points where they go, and a note says it in words.

If there are no moves left on the table, Hint points to the deck. If the hand is truly stuck, it says so — try Undo, or deal a fresh hand.

Finish it for me

Near the end of a hand, when the computer can see that the rest is certain to come out, a Finish it for me button appears. Click it and the game plays out the last moves for you.

Show Me How

In Other Games → Show Me How (or the Show Me How button on the How to Play page), the game plays a whole hand by itself, slowly, explaining each kind of move as it goes. Click Stop the Demonstration whenever you're ready to play. A demonstration never counts in your record.

The Menu

Back to the Table, Deal a Different Game, Restart This Hand (same cards from the beginning — it doesn't count as a loss), Piano Requests, The Ledger, Spider Family Album, Settings, How to Play and Leave the Parlor.

Nothing is lost by accident Whenever something would end a hand you've started — a new deal, a different game — the game asks first.

6. Playing on a Phone

On a phone the parlor rearranges itself so the cards can be as big as possible. (On a tablet you get the full parlor with the painted wall, just like on a computer.)

The phone table: score, buttons and clock along the top; finished suits and the deck in a lane on the right.

  • Hold the phone sideways. The game always plays in landscape.
  • Tap a card to move it to the best spot, or drag it with your finger.
  • Tap the deck on the right to deal.
  • The phone's Back button closes whatever is open — a menu, a screen — or opens the menu when you're at the table. On the welcome screen it asks whether you want to leave.
  • Settings, the Ledger, How to Play and the Album scroll: slide a finger up and down on them.
  • When choosing a photo, pinch with two fingers to zoom (or use the − and + buttons).
  • A phone has no room for the painted wall, so there's no window, cat, lamps, Wanted poster or broom. Switch to the Back Room in Settings, and find the Wanted hands under the Ledger's Ride Out or in Other Games. To leave, press Back on the welcome screen.

7. Which Game to Play

GameWhat's different
Spider, 1 Suit (Easy)All 104 cards are spades. Any run is a same-suit run, so groups move freely. The best place to start.
Spider, 2 Suits (Medium)Spades and hearts. Now you have to sort the colours out.
Spider, 3 Suits (Harder)Spades, hearts and diamonds.
Spider, 4 Suits (Hardest)All four suits — the real test.
SpideretteOne deck, seven columns — a shorter, quicker game, in 1, 2 or 4 suits (with 3 picked it deals 2). Under Other Games.
Relaxed SpiderThe same as Spider, but you may deal even when a column is empty. Under Other Games.

Winnable hands only

With this switched on (it is, to begin with), every hand you're dealt has been solved by the computer first — thousands of them, for every kind of game. If a hand seems impossible, it isn't: try Undo, a Hint, or Restart This Hand. Switch it off on the welcome screen for a completely random shuffle.

Bigger, clearer cards

In Settings you can choose Large Print faces, Extra-Large cards (on a computer), four-colour suits (blue diamonds, green clubs) and High contrast. See chapter 13.

8. Playing the Same Hand as Family

Every deal has a hand number, shown at the bottom right of the table. Same number plus same number of suits means exactly the same cards — so you can race the family on the same hand, no internet needed.

Play a Hand #

  1. On the welcome screen, click Play a Hand #.
  2. Type the number (for example 48213) and pick the suits.
  3. Click Deal It. Then compare your scores and times.

Hand of the Day

Each day there's one special hand for each number of suits. Everyone who plays the Hand of the Day with the same suits gets the same cards that day — a friendly daily contest. Win it and Professor Octavius Eightlegs may stop by to congratulate you.

Send a Telegram

After a win, click Send a Telegram. The game copies a short message — your game, hand number, time, score and moves, plus a "Can you beat it?" challenge — so you can paste it into a text message or email to the family.

The Wanted poster

A hand you gave up on goes up on the Wanted poster on the wall. Click the poster (or Try #… Again in the Ledger) to deal that exact hand again. Win it and you collect a $100 bounty — and, the first time, the Bounty Collected brass plaque.

The Wanted Hand of the Day — $250

Every day one of the toughest winnable hands in town is wanted, matched to the number of suits you play. Everyone who plays the same suits gets the same outlaw that day. Bring it in for a $250 bounty and the Outlaw of the Day plaque. Find it on the Wanted poster (when no other outlaws are at large), in the Ledger right beside the Wanted hand (Ride Out), and in Other Games.

9. When You Win

The cards bounce across the felt with confetti

Sometimes a member of the Spider Family drops by

Big days make the front page of the Parlor Gazette

The win card: your score, time and moves

Every win in the parlor gets the bouncing-card celebration, a hoedown on the piano, and a sepia "tintype" photograph for your Scrapbook (the latest 60 are kept). Click anywhere to hurry it along. In the Back Room a win ends with a gold cascade instead.

Now and then — never every time — a spider comes to visit, and the first time you meet one they join your Spider Family Album. On special days the Parlor Gazette prints an extra edition: your first win, a new record, a promotion, a win milestone (10, 25, 50, 100 wins and on), a new parlor decoration, a bounty collected, the Outlaw of the Day, your first four-suit win, the Hand of the Day.

The win card lists your score, time and moves, any new brass plaques, and gives you three choices: Deal Again, Replay This Hand, or Send a Telegram — or go back to the Main Menu.

The music

The song plate under the sign (number 13 in chapter 4) shows what's playing. Original ragtime, waltzes and cakewalks play on the player piano, with gentler tunes in the evening and a hoedown for every win. It never plays three of the same kind of tune in a row. Turn it up, down or off in Settings.

  • Skip Song moves straight on to the next song. For 20 seconds afterwards the plate offers Don't Play It Again — click it and that song is taken out of your playlist — or Never Mind to close the offer.
  • Changed your mind? Settings → Skipped Songs lists every song you've taken out, each with Put Back in Playlist (and Put Them All Back when there's more than one).
  • Pick a Song (or the player piano on the wall, or Piano Requests in the Menu) lets you choose a song, and it starts right away.
  • On a phone, the ♪ Skip button is in the top bar.

Mose, and the face cards

Three quiet minutes, and the Kings, Queens and Jacks nod off

Mose greets you by name, polishes his glasses and pipes up now and then. Make him Chatty, Quiet or Silent in Settings.

Leave the game alone for three minutes and the face cards that are showing close their eyes and let their heads droop, one by one, with little "z"s floating up (Frontier and Classic faces; Large Print faces stay awake). Move the mouse or touch anything and they wake up.

And for anyone who can't stand spiders: there's a secret somewhere in the parlor. Look around.

The parlor grows, holidays and the train

Your winnings decorate the parlor: a spittoon at 10 wins, longhorns at 25, a chandelier at 50 and a gramophone at 100 (click it for Piano Requests). With Holidays on, the parlor dresses up for Valentine's Day, the Fourth of July, Halloween, Thanksgiving and Christmas; with The 4:10 train on, a whistle sounds on the hour and a little train crosses the window (not in the Back Room or with Parlor life on Still).

14. Your Own Photos on the Cards

Put a family photo, a pet or a favourite place on the back of every card. Go to Settings → Use a Photo… (once you've added one it's called My Photos…) and follow three steps.

Step 1 — Choose a Photo…

1. Choose a photo

Click Choose a Photo… and pick a picture. On a phone, your phone's own photo picker opens.

Step 2 — slide and zoom

2. Slide & zoom

Drag the photo so the best part sits inside the card shape. Use − and + (or pinch on a phone) to zoom. Is the picture lying on its side? Click Turn the Photo. The little card on the right shows how it will look. Click Next.

Step 3 — pick a style

3. Pick a style

Natural colour, Old-Time Sepia or Tintype, with a Gold, Wood or no border. Click Done.

Add as many photos as you like. Turn on Family Album to get a different photo on each new hand. To take a photo off the cards, open My Photos… and click Remove under it — the picture on your computer or phone itself is never touched.

15. The Back Room

Sometimes you just want the cards. The Back Room hides the parlor, the visitors and Mose, and keeps the handsome table, the cards and the smooth animations. Wins end with an elegant gold cascade instead of the full celebration.

Switch it on or off with the little door at the bottom of the table, or with Play in the Back Room at the top of Settings. Your record and everything else stays exactly the same.

16. Players and the Guest Book

Several people can share the game, and each keeps their own name, saved hand, Ledger, plaques and Spider Family Album.

  • Add someone: on the welcome screen click Not Mr. Perry? (it shows the current name). With one name in the book the Guest Book opens for the new name; with more, pick + Someone New. Or use Settings → Guest Book → Add a Player.
  • Switch players: click Not Mr. Perry? and pick a name. Your own hand is put away safely and comes back when you return.
  • Change a name: Settings → Guest Book → Change Name.

18. Questions and Answers

Will I lose my game if I close it?

No. The game saves after every move. Next time, click Continue Your Game. (The one exception is a secret: if you ever Rage Quit, that hand counts as one that got away.)

This hand looks impossible.

With Winnable hands only on, every hand can be won. Try Hint, use Undo to back up a few moves and try another way, or Menu → Restart This Hand to start the same cards fresh (that doesn't count as a loss).

The cards or writing are too small.

In Settings choose Large Print faces, Extra-Large cards and High contrast, and turn on Full screen.

Too much is moving around.

Turn on Gentle motion, set Parlor life to Still, or play in The Back Room.

I don't like one of the songs.

Click Skip Song on the plate under the sign, then Don't Play It Again. Bring it back any time in Settings → Skipped Songs.

I can't hear any music.

Check Settings → Music is On and the slider isn't all the way down, and that your computer or phone isn't muted.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC."

Click More info, then Run anyway. It appears because the game is from a small developer, not because anything is wrong.

It says "Perry's Parlor is already open."

The game is already running — look for it on the taskbar at the bottom of the screen.

How do I update it?

On Windows, run the new Setup; everything you've done is kept. On Android, the Play Store updates it for you.

How do I uninstall it?

On Windows: Settings → Apps, find Perry's Parlor, click Uninstall. Your records are left behind in case you reinstall; to remove them too, delete the folder %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Perry's Parlor. On Android: press and hold the gold star, then Uninstall (this removes everything).

19. Privacy and Credits

Your privacy

Perry's Parlor collects nothing. There's no account, no advertising, no tracking, and the game itself never uses the internet. Names, scores, settings and any photos you add stay on your own computer or phone. (On a computer, the About the Parlor page has two buttons about FaleOfaz, a care home in Utah. They open your own web browser, and only if you click them. Nothing is asked of you.) The full policy is at tcdeveloper27.github.io/perrys-parlor/privacy.html.
